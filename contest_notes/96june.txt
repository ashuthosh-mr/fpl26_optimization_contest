## PhysOpt vs RapidWright Placement Experiments

### Objective

Evaluate whether RapidWright placement optimizations can provide additional gains after Vivado physical optimization.

### Baseline Results

| Flow                 | Approx. Fmax |
| -------------------- | ------------ |
| Original benchmark   | ~311 MHz     |
| Vivado phys_opt flow | ~411 MHz     |

Vivado physical optimization alone improved performance significantly, increasing Fmax from approximately 311 MHz to 411 MHz.

---

### Experiment 1 - Manual MUL Register Move

Cell:

```text
memory_to_writeBack_MUL_LOW_reg[35]
```

Result:

```text
SLICE_X124Y12 → SLICE_X125Y12
```

After routing and timing analysis:

```text
WNS: -0.862 ns → -0.910 ns
```

Timing worsened.

---

### Experiment 2 - Manual RegFile LUT Move

Cell:

```text
dataCache_1/RegFilePlugin_regFile_reg_r1_0_31_28_31_i_3
```

Result:

```text
SLICE_X115Y15 → SLICE_X112Y18
```

The move was significantly larger than the MUL register experiment, indicating that RapidWright believed the cell was misplaced.

After routing and timing analysis:

```text
WNS ≈ -0.910 ns
```

No improvement was observed.

---

### Experiment 3 - Full run_test_vexriscv Flow on 411 MHz Design

The contest-provided `run_test_vexriscv` flow was executed on the 411 MHz design.

Selected cell:

```text
dataCache_1/ways_0_tags_reg_i_22
```

Placement change:

```text
SLICE_X115Y14 → SLICE_X113Y22
```

Distance moved:

```text
9 tiles
```

After routing:

```text
Initial WNS: -0.862 ns
Final WNS:   -1.133 ns

Initial Fmax: 411.18 MHz
Final Fmax:   369.96 MHz

Change: -41.22 MHz
```

This was the largest degradation observed.

### Key Observation

The RapidWright placement optimization strategy that improves the original benchmark (311 MHz → ~320-350 MHz) does not provide additional benefit after Vivado physical optimization has already improved the design to ~411 MHz.

All placement experiments performed after the phys_opt stage either produced no improvement or significantly degraded timing.

### Current Hypothesis

The timing bottleneck present in the original benchmark appears to be largely addressed by Vivado physical optimization. The remaining timing violations in the ~411 MHz design are likely caused by a different bottleneck than the one targeted by the contest's centroid-based placement optimization flow.

Future work should focus on characterizing the post-phys_opt critical paths rather than continuing single-cell placement experiments.
